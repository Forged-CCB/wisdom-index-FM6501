How sermons are built, and how the app could build them

Most methods are variations on one move: find the single idea the text is saying, name the human need it meets, then shape the message so listeners travel from that need to God's answer. The app should offer these shapes as templates, not write the sermon for you.

Sermon types differ by how tightly they stay with the text

The classic split from John Broadus is topical, textual and expository, and later writers treat these as a gradient rather than hard boxes. Narrative and other forms overlap with all three. Many preachers argue a good teacher moves between them.

Six prep methods worth turning into templates

Each one gives a different first question. The app can let you pick one per lesson, or combine them (for example Big Idea for the core sentence plus Four Pages for the flow).

Bible study methods cover the solo and group side

These are the same engine as sermon prep, run for yourself or a table of men instead of a room. They map straight to the app's Solo and Discuss modes.

Prep works best in a cycle with simmer time

One pastor writing for Leadership Journal starts text work ten days out, writes down every question he can't yet answer, and lets those questions steer his reading and thinking for the rest of the week. Another, writing for Crossway, works in five steps: text work, secondary reading, structuring, writing, and getting familiar with the message, with six to eight hours on the text alone.

Your current Forged rhythm (capture all week, compile Friday to Monday, slides, then PDF and podcast script) already follows this pattern. The app should mirror it rather than replace it.

Suggested app phases

  1. Plan: pick passage, type and method; set teach date.
  2. Study: read, observe, cross-references, word studies, open questions list.
  3. Distill: write the big idea (subject + complement) and the human need.
  4. Shape: arrange blocks from the chosen method.
  5. Write: lesson text, illustrations, application, slide markers.
  6. Rehearse: timing, delivery cues, practice run.
  7. Teach and export: teach view, then PDF, HTML, .docx, slides outline, podcast script.

Delivery: most preachers carry something, few read it

Public-speaking texts list four methods: manuscript, memorized, extemporaneous (from notes or an outline) and impromptu. Preaching writers add the "digested manuscript": write it all out, absorb it, then preach without it.

Speaking tips the app can actually coach

    Block builder: write in pieces, rearrange, then stitch

    Each section of a lesson is its own block (text, story, explanation, application, question, slide). Grab the handle and drag a block anywhere, the way Notion moves a whole section; arrow buttons do the same for keyboard users. Blocks can also be grouped under a section heading, so dragging the heading carries every block beneath it. Between every pair sits a transition slot.

    A transition belongs to the pair of blocks around it. When you move a block, any pair that's new gets flagged for review, and the old transition is kept in case the pair comes back together. A review pass then walks you through each seam.

    Transition kinds to offer

    • Review and preview: sum up what was said, point to what's next.
    • Question: raise the question the next block answers.
    • Big idea restatement: repeat the core sentence in new words.
    • Turn: move from trouble to grace, or from then to now.
    • Signpost: a plain marker ("Second, ...").

    AI can draft a transition from the two blocks on either side; you edit or reject it.
